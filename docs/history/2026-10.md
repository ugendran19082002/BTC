## Strategies in groups: one per account to start, cloned across accounts (10 Oct 2026)

Owner: "group strategy ... existing account 1 one group, account 2 another group ... clone, update, shuffle
between account 1 and 2 ... not affect logic, safe update". Decided with the owner: a group's switch sets each
strategy's own (the runner is unchanged); a group is one account's, any number per account; a clone is copied
switched off with live orders off; the first start makes one group per account.

- **DB**: `strategy_groups` (name unique per account, case-blind) and `strategies.group_id`, no foreign key, as
  the rest of the schema. Migration `strategy-010-groups` is additive and idempotent: on a read-only copy of the
  live database it made "High Win%" (14 strategies) and "Low win%" (6), every switch as it was.
- **Server** (`strategy.routes.ts`): make, rename, all on/off (each by `validateConfig`, refusals listed in
  `leftOff`), clone to an account, delete (strategies kept), move within the account. Saving a strategy never
  changes its group; its own Copy stays in it.
- **Screen**: the Signal Strategies card lists by group -- fold, "3 of 5 on", Turn all on (two taps) / off,
  rename, clone to account, delete (two taps), New strategy in the group; each card as it was, with a Group
  picker. The phone's Strategies screen lists by group.
- Tests: `test/http/strategy-groups.test.ts` (7), `StrategyGroups.test.tsx` (8), the phone's (2).

ui backend db". Measured on a local paper desk over a read-only copy of the live database (1,316 trades, both

## Audit: the Live screen's paint, two indexes, the phone's bundle, two lists for screen readers (9 Oct 2026)

A full audit (owner: "code + db + ui full audit ... very smooth ... best practice") on a local paper desk on live
data, and read-only on the live database. Both suites passed but for the stale `files.md` (fixed by `npm run
docs`). What was found, and changed:

- **The Live screen was not smooth.** 48 fps on a fast CPU, 6 fps on one a quarter as fast; the main thread was
  busy a third of the time. Two causes. Every print of the perpetual (several a second) re-rendered both method
  tables and the legend -- 27,200 cells in 10 s -- because the reads were filtered inline and the tables were not
  memoised; and the chain re-rendered all its rows on every App render, because its `onFocus` was an inline
  arrow. Now `MethodTable` and `MethodLegend` are `memo`, their props kept (`singleReads`, `mtfReads`,
  `useCallback`), and the board gets `focusLeg`: 2,720 cells in 10 s. The bigger cost was paint: each changed
  number re-recorded the whole 6,700px page. `content-visibility: auto` on the entry setups and the chain
  (styles.css) paints each block on its own and skips one off-screen: paint 980 -> 59 ms per 5 s, 56 fps, long
  tasks -87%, 16 fps on the slow CPU. What is left is `App` re-rendering on each print -- the refactor of `App`.
- **Two indexes on `entry_setups`** (`entry-022-setups-by-time`). The log is never pruned; the board's setup
  clocks ask by `trigger_at` alone, the last column of the unique key, so each 5 s read walked the whole index
  (~11 ms on 35k rows), and the latest setups sorted on `first_seen` with no index (75 ms).
- **The phone downloaded the chart library.** `lightweight-charts` sat in the catch-all `vendor` chunk, which
  /m loads for `cn()`; it has its own `charts` chunk now -- the phone's vendor 76 -> 22 KB gzipped.
- **fastify 5.12.3 -> 5.12.5, fast-uri** (`npm audit fix`, lockfile only): an HTTP/2 DoS the desk, on HTTP/1
  behind nginx, was not exposed to.
- **Screen readers.** The account card's figures were one `<dl>` around tiles, rules, tabs and buttons; each
  figure is its own `<dl>` now, and the limit's Edit button sits beside its list. The P&L calendar's week total
  is a `gridcell`, as its row requires. The phone had no axe violation; the desk's colour contrast (480, most on
  Live) is left for a palette change the owner sees first.

Left open: the contrast; `pg_stat_statements` is preloaded but not created, so the 402k sequential scans of
`trade_events` (1.07 billion rows read, on 8k rows) have no named query yet; `App` (773 lines) and the trade
and desk route files are the riskiest code by complexity and churn.

## "SL is not 300%": the screens showed the backstop as the stop, and the backstop sat past the close-out (8 Oct 2026, night)

The owner, on the desk and the phone: a 61 sale showed "Stop 425.40 -> lose Rs 160", a 73 sale "SL 439.00 (314%)",
against a 300% setting. The stops themselves were right -- 243.2 and 256, the second held inside the close-out --
but neither screen showed them.

- **The screens read the order resting at Delta.** Since 29 Sep that order is the *backstop*, further out than the
  stop (`backstopFor`: the stop plus its distance from the entry), because Delta triggers on the mark and the mark
  spikes on thin options; the stop itself is judged by the desk, on the offer. The desk's card and the phone's rail
  both took the resting order's price and called it the stop. Now both show the stop the desk judges, with its share
  of the entry -- `Stop 243.20 (299%) -> lose Rs 79 · backstop at Delta 253.40` -- and what it leaves
  (`ifExits.deskStop`, new from the server). A stop not resting at Delta still reads "none", with the warning.
- **The backstop could never fire.** 243.2 + 182.2 = 425.4, past the 265 close-out: the exchange closes the
  position first. That was true of every 300% stop since the backstop began -- a $50 sale's backstop (350) was
  past its 253 close-out too. Now the backstop sits a twentieth of the room inside the close-out, worked from BTC
  at the trade's entry (fixed for the trade, as the margin posted is), and never under the stop
  (`closeOutOf`, `BACKSTOP_INSIDE_CLOSE_OUT` in `trading/engine.ts`). A backstop already inside is left as it was.

**Correction, the same night.** I wrote that the next check after the deploy would move the stops already resting.
It did not: the desk re-places protection only when an order is missing or covers the wrong size, and a stop at the
wrong *level* was neither -- the 30 open trades kept their backstops past the close-out. Now the level a stop rests at
is written down with it (`ProtectionOrders.stopAt`, on `protection_placed`), and a stop resting anywhere other than
`backstopOf` the trade is re-placed (`missingProtection`); a record from before the level was kept is re-placed once
and then carries it, so nothing is placed again on every poll (`stop-orders.test.ts`).

Order path in a worktree, landed with one `git apply`. Tests: `stop-orders` (the cap), `PositionsCard` and
`position-risk` (the screens); then both full suites.

## A perp stop touched and let go: a level that trades is reached (8 Oct 2026, night)

Checking a week of signal exits against the perp's own prices: 450 of them closed on the perp's stop or target,
every one with the perp at or through the level -- but 35 closed more than three minutes after the perp first
traded through it, most on 2-4 Oct, before the fast watch (4 Oct). Two came after it. On 5 Oct at 11:49 the perp
printed 85,997.50 through a SELL signal's stop at 85,990.79 -- the desk's own tape recorded that high -- and was back
inside within the second. The fast watch saw it and handed the trade over; by the time the trade's turn came the
last trade was back inside, and the engine's re-check closed nothing. The trade stayed open until 12:17, bought back
at 82 where it was sold at 39. On 6 Oct the same, three points through.

**Why.** The desk asked "is the *last* trade through the level?" at the moment it looked. A stop is a trigger: an
exchange's stop order fires on the trade that reaches it and does not ask again a second later.

**What changed** (`underlyingTouched`, `UNDERLYING_LOOKBACK_MS` in `trading/engine.ts`; `perpRange` in
`market/flow.ts`):

- Every look at a signal trade's perp levels reads the highest and lowest the perp traded since the last two
  minutes, off the desk's own tape -- never a print from before the option filled. Any trade at or through the stop
  is the stop; through the target, the target; both in one stretch is the stop.
- The fast watch hands the engine the print it saw with the trade, so a level reached a moment before the engine's
  turn still counts.
- Unchanged: nothing is acted on without a fresh last trade (a stopped feed is no evidence), the engine still runs it
  in the trade's own queue (a trade already closing is not closed twice), and the perp is still judged before the
  option's own stop.
- The reason says when it traded if that was not the last trade: `BTC perp at 85,997.50 reached the signal's stop
  85,990.79 (traded 11:49:03 IST; 85,958.00 now)`.

Order path: built in a separate worktree, landed with one `git apply`. Tests: the pure question, 5 Oct replayed on
the engine, the window's limits, the watch's hand-over, a stale feed; then the full server suite.

## A 300% stop refused every delta trade; a share stop is now held inside the close-out (8 Oct 2026, night)

The evening the first delta strategies went live, every signal they took was refused:

    sell CE 83200 x5 @ 70 (delta 0.10) · perp filled 81026 · perp SL 82962 · TGT 79000
      -- refused: Stop at 304.00 is past the 278.53 close-out at 200x — you would be liquidated before it fired.

**Why.** The strategies carry an option stop of 300% (`stopLossPct: 3`): buy back at four times the entry. At 200x
a short is closed out by the exchange a fixed distance over what it was sold for -- half of BTC over the leverage,
about $203 with BTC at 81,000 -- whatever it was sold for (`liquidationPrice` in `trading/margin.ts`). 300% of a
$50 premium is $150 over the entry and fits; 300% of the $76 offer here is $228 over and does not. The premium rule
had capped entries at $50, so the same stop had always fitted; "delta at or under 0.10" sold $65-78 strikes on its
first evening and none of them did. The gate (`STOP_BEYOND_LIQUIDATION`) was right that such a stop is not a stop,
and wrong for a strategy in what it did about it: an unattended strategy refused every signal of the night.

**What changed.** A strategy's stop asked for as a share or as points is held to the last level that is still a
stop: nine tenths of the close-out's room over the entry (`ExitAsk.stopMaxPoints`, `stopRoomInside`,
`STOP_INSIDE_CLOSE_OUT` in `trading/order-plan.ts`; set by `placeArgs` in `strategy/runner.ts` from BTC's price at
the default leverage every strategy order goes out at). The tenth kept back is for what the desk's own stop takes
to act -- it judges the offer, held fifteen seconds -- while the exchange closes out on the mark.

- It only ever tightens: a stop already inside the room is left exactly as asked, to the tick.
- It follows the fill with the stop it holds (`anchorExits`): offered at 76 and filled at 78, the stop is 78 plus
  the hold, not back out at 312.
- It stays with a stop moved later as a share or points (the strategy's time steps, the edit sheet), and goes
  when a stop is typed as a price by hand -- that is a level somebody named.
- A stop typed as a price, and every order from the ticket, are judged as before: the ticket still answers
  "past the close-out", because a person is there to read it.
- The trade's own line says it where it happened: `· option SL 258.3 (asked 304, held inside the close-out)`.

The trade's real exits are unchanged: the signal's SL and TGT on the perp, and the option's take-profit.

Order path: built in a separate worktree and landed with one `git apply`. Tests: the arithmetic, the engine on
the paper exchange (refused as asked, placed when held, the stop after a fill above the ticket, later moves), and
a signal end to end (`exits-follow-fill.test.ts`, `signal-strategy.test.ts`); then both full suites.

## Two more strike rules: by delta, and by distance from BTC (8 Oct 2026)

The owner asked for them beside the two the form had ("by premium, by strike already there -- by delta, by
distance, user friendly: UI, backend, database"), after a day of comparing ways to pick a strike.

- **By delta** (`strikeRule: "delta"`, `delta: { max }`): the strike nearest the money whose delta is at or under
  the number, read from the live board when the signal arrives. A strike whose delta the board could not work out
  is passed over, never sold as if it were far away.
- **By distance** (`strikeRule: "distance"`, `distance: { pct, scale }`): the strike nearest the money at least
  that far from BTC. `fixed` is the percentage as it stands; `time` multiplies it by the square root of the hours
  left to the settlement, so 0.45 asks for 2.0% in the evening and 0.6% two hours before 5:30 PM. Without BTC's
  price, or without the time left when it needs it, it picks nothing.
- Both are out of the money only, have no else strike and no "if none", and are still held to the minimum
  premium. They work as the one rule of a window or as a block's rule, and "Apply to all blocks" copies them.
- **The form** (`form-parts.tsx` `DeltaFields`, `DistanceFields`; `StrikeBlocksEditor.tsx`): four choices where
  there were two; a rule switched to starts at the figure that was measured (0.10; 0.45 with the time left); a
  distance says under itself what it comes to, in percent and in points, and on a block over that block's hours.
- **No migration**: `config` is JSONB and both keys are written only when sent, so a strategy that uses neither
  is stored, and picks, exactly as before (pinned in `select.test.ts` and `signal-strategy.test.ts`).

**What they rest on**, and the form says it: replayed on the 699 real sell orders of 1-8 Oct (same entry, side,

## The SL and TGT range for a strategy with the timeframe chain (6 Oct 2026)

"Take a signal only if its SL and TGT are in range" was a block for strategies without the chain; with the chain
there was none, and the numbers were not read. It is there now: one row, **Chain**, with the same four numbers --
SL at least and at most, TGT at least and at most, in BTC points from the perp entry -- and the same skipped row in
the history ("this strategy takes timeframe-chain signals only at 400 pts or more").

**The trap avoided.** The obvious way was to read the 5m number with the chain, since the chain enters on 5m. But
the live "All time frame" strategy is with the chain and still carries `{"5m": 100, "4h": 100}` on both its SL and
its TGT, from the strategy it was copied from -- never read. Reading them would have switched a filter on in a
live strategy that nobody set. So the chain's numbers are their own key, `chain`, in the same four maps

## 2h, a timeframe like the rest (6 Oct 2026)

**Why.** Asked whether to add 2h and 1D, the paper log and the real trades of 1-6 Oct were read by timeframe
(without the chain). Both get better as the bars get longer:

| tf | paper trades | TGT1 | avg R | median hold | real SELL trades | real win |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3m | 7,200 | 35% | -0.12 | 0.2 h | -- | -- |
| 5m | 4,912 | 37% | -0.07 | 0.3 h | 38 | 55% |
| 15m | 1,792 | 43% | +0.08 | 0.7 h | 188 | 65% |
| 30m | 1,033 | 48% | +0.19 | 1.5 h | 108 | 76% |
| 1h | 604 | 51% | +0.27 | 3.0 h | 75 | 79% |
| 4h | 151 | 56% | +0.42 | 5.8 h | 23 | 87% |

Five days, one regime, and 4h's 151 trades are 29 bars with about five methods on each -- thin. 2h sits between
the two best and gives twelve bars a day to 4h's six, so it was added. **1D was not**: the options expire the same
day and a strategy's window is a day, while a third of 4h paper trades already hold over twelve hours and one in
ten over thirty; a daily bar is one new bar a day, months to judge. It belongs as a filter, not an entry.

**What changed.** `2h` in the timeframe lists: `Tf` / `TF_SEC`, `SINGLE_TFS`, the venue frames (`read.ts` -- the
2h candles were already in the series every read asks for, so no request is added), `ALERT_TFS`, the history's
order, `SIGNAL_TFS`, the chart's candles; on the web the same lists, and the entry chart folds 2h from the hourly
bars (it folded anything past 1h to 4h). Nothing else: the 81 methods, gates, stops, targets, paper rules and
alerts read whatever timeframe they are given. 2h is **not** a step of the chain.

**What it must not change, and how that is held.**

- *No existing read*: no method or gate walks the frames -- each asks for its own by name. Tested: with 2h candles
  trending the opposite way, every method's read on 3m-4h and with the chain is identical to without them.
- *No existing timing*: 2h does not gate the early look after a close (`NOT_GATING`); a 2h candle the venue is
  slow with is left out of that run (`finalAt`, `withoutClosing`) and read on the next pass.
- *No strategy by itself*: each strategy keeps its own list of timeframes, and all seven live ones name theirs.
  A 2h signal is traded only by a strategy made or edited to take 2h. Alerts go to the timeframes picked, as before.
- *No measurable cost*: 648 reads a minute for 567, about 23 ms either way.

Built in a worktree, landed with `git apply --check` first. Run once on live candles from the worktree: 219 2h
candles, 81 reads, 3 TRADE and 1 WAIT written to its own history. Server 1,440 (4 new), web 1,101 (2 new).

## A bought position's target rests at Delta; its exits edited as a long's (5 Oct 2026)

**What broke.** A BUY strategy's first real order (account 2, 85,600 PE, 1 @ 40, target 350%) showed
"target none". The target was kept (`plan.longExits`) but nothing rested at Delta: the desk sold at the market
when the bid reached it, which works only while the desk is up. Worse, the Edit exits sheet treated the long as
a short -- "Sold 1", "between 0 and 99% of the credit", a liquidation price -- and saving it would have written the
short's `takeProfitPrice` / `stopPrice` onto the long, where `stopIfReached` judges a short's stop on the offer
and could have sold a winner at the market.

**The wrong assumption.** That a bought option needs nothing resting because its loss is bounded. Bounded is not
the same as watched: the target is the exit somebody asked for, and it has to work with the desk down.

**What changed.**

- `protectLong` (engine): a long's target rests as a reduce-only limit **sale** at the target off the price paid,
  rounded down to the tick and never at or under the entry; the book is read first, an order at the wrong price is
  edited in place, a cancel is verified before a replacement. `missingProtection` knows a long wants it. The stop
  still rests nowhere: the desk judges it on the bid for 15 s. The desk's own market sale at the target now runs
  only when nothing rests for it (placement refused).
- Edit exits on a long (`updateLongExits`, `longExitsFor`): the same request mirrored -- a target a share over the
  price paid with no 99% cap, a stop under it at most 100%, or prices on the right side; the short's fields stay
  null. The sheet has a long's own form (`LongExitsForm`).
- Positions and Orders read a long as one: "Bought 1 @ 40", "Buying" on a working buy, no liquidation, leverage
  or "premium earned", the desk's stop named, "Bought at / Sold at" and the long's target and stop in the detail,
  the entry fill labelled "Bought". "If closed now" for a long is priced at the bid.
- Charges, long and short: one formula for both sides (fee = min(0.01% of notional, 3.5% of premium) + 18% GST),
  checked against Delta's own commission on every fill of the day -- SELL 200 of 200 exact, BUY 2 of 2 exact
  ($0.001652 on the 1 @ 40). "To close" on a card is now priced where the close would trade -- the bid for a long,
  the ask for a short -- the same price as "If closed now" beside it; it was the mark.
- Live after the deploy: both open longs on account 2 got their target at Delta -- reduce-only sales of 1 at 180
  (bought at 40) and at 207 (bought at 46), 350% over, read back from Delta's order book.

## A long limit beside the short limit, and the room left to sell and buy (5 Oct 2026)

## A BUY strategy places real buy orders (5 Oct 2026)

The owner: "the BUY account's strategies must run and take real trades, the way the SELL account's do." Until now
a BUY strategy was written down only, because the engine could only sell to open.

- **Bought to open, sold to close** (`engine.ts`): the entry is a buy -- a limit at the offer, so it crosses and pays
  no more than the price it was judged at, cancelled if unfilled after `SIGNAL_ENTRY_MS`; every close of a bought
  position is a reduce-only sale (perp SL/TGT, the window's end, a manual close). No leverage is set for a buy.
- **The record** (`machine.ts`): `isLong` reads the entry fills; a long's position counts up and its profit is sold
  less paid. Open P&L, the close preview, the day's booked P&L and the order words follow it. Every short's
  arithmetic is unchanged.

  premium plus fee in the free balance, the day's loss budget with the premium as the worst case -- and never on a
  contract the account holds short, nor a sale on one it holds bought (Delta nets a contract's position).
- **Its own target and stop** (`plan.longExits`, `longExitIfReached`): nothing rests at Delta for a long; the desk
  judges both on the bid each poll -- the target at once, the stop after `STOP_CONFIRM_MS`, as a short's stop on the
  offer. They work while the desk is up, like the perp's levels; the most a bought option can lose is its premium.
  Time steps on a BUY strategy's exits are refused for now (`BUY_NO_STEPS`).
- **Screen**: Live orders is a normal switch for BUY strategies again (two taps on the card: "Tap again: real buys").
- **Checked**: 10 new engine cases (`test/trading/buy-orders.test.ts`) and the signal e2e on a paper desk -- a real
  buy placed, long on the exchange, nothing resting, closed with a sale; both full suites; a real-time run on live

- **The Strategy card on a BUY account** (same evening): its own tiles -- *BUY strategies allow*, *In use now*,

## Faster to the order, and to the signal: six changes that trade nothing differently (4 Oct 2026)

The owner asked for the entry caught sooner. Each of these removes a wait; none changes which signal is taken,
which strike is sold, or at what price.

- **The leverage is not asked for before every entry.** It is a setting on the product at Delta, and the desk
  set it in front of each order -- a whole round trip to set the number it had set a minute before. It is now
  remembered per product for ten minutes (`LEVERAGE_HELD_MS`), and forgotten the moment an entry is refused.
  With a fallback, because a remembered number is trust and Delta offers no read of it that is cheaper than
  setting it: an entry that went on the remembered leverage has it set again *behind* the order
  (`reassertLeverage`) -- so a change made by hand at Delta is put back before the next entry, and if Delta
  refuses, the memory is dropped and written down and the next entry asks first, refused in the open as before.
- **A contract is made ready before its order is due** (`engine.warm`, the runner's `warmFor`): when a signal
  appears that a strategy will take at the zone, the contract it would sell now has its product looked up and its
  leverage set. Nothing is claimed or placed; the strike is still chosen on the board as it stands when the zone
  fills.
- **A working entry is not left to the loop** (`trading/entry-watch.ts`): a trade with its entry order working,
  or filled and without its target yet, is polled four times a second on its own -- at most two trades a look, and
  none while the desk has used half of Delta's quota. (A correction on the way: the journal's fill time is when the
  desk noticed the fill, not Delta's own, so the 1.6 s measured from fill to target was the target routine and
  its retry -- Delta, asked a moment after a fill, sometimes does not show the position yet -- not the poll.)
- **The perp's own prints drive the watchers** (`onPerpPrint`): the SL and TGT watch looks on every print as well
  as every 100 ms, and the zone grader on every print, not more than once in 50 ms.
- **The signal run goes as soon as the candle is final** (the owner's sequence: close, wait a second, read, verify,
  run, or try again shortly). It waited three seconds after every close. Now it looks at one second and at two,
  and runs at once if the venue's candles are exactly what the desk's own tape says the minute was -- close,
  high, low and volume, and every longer candle closing on the same boundary ending at the same trade
  (`closeVerified`). Otherwise the settled read at three seconds runs, as before. The methods read the venue's
  candles either way.
- **Checked in real time** on a paper copy reading Delta's live data: the venue's 1m candle was exactly the
  tape's minute one second after the close on four minutes of four (close, high, low and volume), and the signal
  run went early on three of five minutes -- the first two could not, the tape having only just connected. Signals
  were ready about 2 s after the close on an early run, against 4.1 s on the settled one. The run's own
  calculation held the thread for 0.14 to 0.23 s.
- **Not built**: a thread of its own for the signal run (the gauges now say how long it holds the thread; it is
  decided on that), and Delta's private fill stream (it cannot be proven here without the account's keys).

## A stop was acted on late: the fast watch on a signal trade's SL and TGT (4 Oct 2026)

The owner asked for speed on the signal path. Measured first, on the day's own records:

| Step | Median | Worst decile |
|---|---|---|
| Candle closes -> signal seen | 3.0 s | by design: the read runs 3 s after the close |
| Signal seen -> order sent (every strategy enters at the zone) | 7.9 s | 239 s, waiting for the perp to reach the zone |
| Order sent -> first fill | 21 s | 48 s: the order rests at the offer and holds at the mid while the spread is over 15% |
| Fill -> target resting at Delta | 1.6 s | 2.3 s |
| Close sent -> filled | 1.0 s | 1.5 s |
| **Perp through the SL -> the desk acts** | **10 pts past** | **82 pts past; 185 at worst** |

The last row was the fault. A signal trade's SL and TGT are perp levels, judged inside the engine's poll -- and the
service polled the open trades one after another, each poll three round trips to Delta. With twenty trades open, a
stop waited behind nineteen trades that had nothing to do: 30 of 95 stops in three days were acted on more than 25
points late, and five trades stopped together at 02:36 on 4 Oct were closed over 19 seconds, 87 to 100 points past.

- **The fast watch** (`trading/underlying-watch.ts`): the perp's last trade, already in memory off the tape, against
  every open signal trade's levels ten times a second. A trade through its level is handed to the engine at once
  (`exitOnUnderlying`): the engine's own check and its own close, in the trade's own queue, so the watch and the
  poll make one close between them. Trades on one contract are closed one after another, as before; different
  contracts at the same time. The poll still judges the same levels on every pass -- if the watch stops, the desk is
  as it was.
- **Two per-second reads held until they can change.** The paper grader's working rows (528 rows, 277 kB, every
  second) are held until the entry tables are written; the day's booked P&L (every trade of the day, every second)
  until the journal is. Both are exact: every writer moves the version the held answer is kept against. Together
  they were most of the 0.8 MB a second the database sent the API.
- **The database given room** (deploy/docker-compose.yml): two CPUs and 1.5 GB instead of one and 512 MB, 512 MB of
  buffers for a 440 MB database, 16 MB a sort, and `pg_stat_statements` loaded. A backup's 18-second COPY had the
  signal path's reads waiting behind it on the one CPU. **The database restarts on the deploy that carries this.**
- **The zone entry sees its fill four times a second.** A strategy that enters "at the zone" sends its option order
  when the live grader sees the perp trade into the zone; the grader looked once a second, so up to a second was
  added to every such entry. It now looks every 250 ms (`LIVE_GRADE_MS`), never two looks at once. The watch on SL
  and TGT looks every 100 ms -- as often as the screens are sent the perp's last trade.
- **Every screen checked at every width.** `app/web/scripts/responsive-check.mjs` now walks all eight screens at
  360, 390, 768, 1024, 1366 and 1920 px and fails if the page scrolls sideways or anything outside a scroller of
  its own reaches past the edge. All pass, on a paper desk with empty tables; its stale "Collapse all" step went.
- **Not changed, and why**: the 3 s wait after a candle closes (the engine reads Delta's finished candle; reading
  the tape instead is a different input and needs measuring first); the 21 s to fill (that is each strategy's own
  "rest at the offer, not into a wide bid" setting); Redis (one process holds its caches in memory -- a second
  server would add a hop, not remove one).

## Delta Exchange lookup optimization & Error logging best practices (3 Oct 2026)

- **Delta direct client ID endpoint (`GET /v2/orders/client_order_id/{id}`)**:
  Orders are now queried directly by Delta's dedicated client ID endpoint first, resolving both open and filled states in a single roundtrip.
- **Contract-specific history using numeric `product_ids`**:
  Deep order search now supplies `product_ids={productId}` (the documented parameter Delta respects) alongside `product_symbols`.
- **Throttling deep order searches (`deepMiss`)**:
  To protect Delta's 20,000 units / 5-min quota, missed deep searches are cached and rate-throttled for 30 seconds (`DEEP_RETRY_MS = 30_000`).
- **Critical fault logging on 5xx (`worthLogging`)**:
  Fixed bug where `worthLogging(500, true)` returned `false`. A 5xx is always an unhandled fault and must never be suppressed from the error log.
- **UI Error Boundary crash loop protection**:
  Added a 2-second retry debounce (`RETRY_COOLDOWN_MS = 2_000`) in `ErrorBoundary.tsx`. If a component crashes immediately after clicking "try again", the boundary locks with a clean user message instead of looping infinitely.
- **Browser error dedup map pruning**:
  Replaced destructive `sent.clear()` on reaching 200 items with TTL-based timestamp pruning in `app/web/src/lib/report-error.ts`, preserving recent fingerprints during error bursts.
- **ErrorLog startup prune**:
  `ErrorLog` now prunes the table on initialization/boot after migrations rather than waiting for 50 writes.

## Twelve trades "closed" at a restart; targets the running desk never saw fill (2 Oct 2026)

The owner: 88,800 CE trades DONE with "bought back at —". Cross-checked on the backups either side of the

## Signal history audit: the paper grader's blind seconds, and its two clocks (1 Oct 2026)

The owner: "signal history R&D". A code audit of how a TRADE is written,
graded and summed (the live rows need a production read this session could
not make). Three grading faults, each biasing the record; fixed going forward.
Rows graded before the fix keep their old result.

- **The rest of an event's minute was graded by nobody.** The live tick grader
  closes the minute of a fill, TP1 or TGT2 to the candle grader (so a candle
  never replays prices from before the event), but then began its own next pass
  at the following minute. A stop seconds after the fill -- the commonest way a
  setup fails -- went unseen, and a later TP1 could be written as the win. A
  runner back to breakeven in its TP1 minute was missed the same way. The tape
  now carries on from its own last trade inside a minute it closed itself
  (`live-grade.ts`); a restart, with no cursor, still never goes back over it.
- **The fill window let one minute too many through**: a setup could fill in
  the minute starting at `fillBy`, after the screen's counter had run out. The
  window is now checked first.
- **The time-out came a minute late**: a stop or TP1 in the minute after the
  hold ran out beat the time-out, which then took that minute's close. Time is
  now judged first, at the open of that minute -- the price when it ran out --
  for the trade and for a runner.

Read the record with two things in mind: R is before fees (`entry-010`), and a
time-out at exactly breakeven counts as a loss in the win rate. Tests:
`entry/live-grade.test.ts` (3 new), `entry/paper.test.ts` (4 new, 1 corrected).

## Exit audit: the losing exit confirmed off the book, the close-watched stop by time (1 Oct 2026)

The owner: "ask, bid, entry, exit, TGT, SL -- best practice, full R&D and
audit". A code audit of where each price is used, how the two exits are kept
in step, and partial fills. Partial fills were already right (exits re-anchor
on the size-weighted fill every poll). Three things were not:

- **The losing exit was struck off the record without being confirmed off the
  book.** `cancelSiblings` wrote `sibling_cancelled` whatever happened -- a
  lookup that failed, a cancel Delta did not honour -- and a flat trade is never
  polled again, so a reduce-only order could rest on with the desk believing it
  gone. With two trades on one contract (0011) it would buy back the other
  trade's contracts. Now a leg comes off the record only once the venue confirms
  it; an unconfirmed one is retried by the trade's poll while it is open and by
  `sweepLeftovers` (every service step) once it is done, and a minute of it is a
  phone alarm. `cancelAndVerify` no longer reads a failed lookup as "gone". A
  close still goes out when its exits cannot be confirmed: a stop that does not
  exit is worse than a reduce-only order resting a moment longer. The list is in
  memory; a restart forgets it.
- **The close-watched stop judged the wrong bar** when no bar was forming:
  `lastClosedBar` took the second-to-last bar on the assumption that the last
  is always the one forming. A thin option with no trade yet this minute has no
  forming bar, so the bar that had just closed through the stop was skipped. It
  now picks the newest bar whose minute is over, by time.
- **The scheduler switch did not say it stops the exit times.** Off now says so
  on the strategy tab, and that the target and stop at Delta still work.
- **An exit could land on the entry price.** A sweep of every entry from 0.1 to
  100 (and half-tick averages) against every percentage and distance found
  rounding to the 0.1 tick putting a target on the entry (any 0.1 entry; any
  distance under a tick) or a stop on it (a 10% stop on 0.1-0.4). A target there
  buys back at the price sold, fees paid twice; a stop there is reached as the
  entry fills. A target now goes to the first tick under the entry, or is none
  at a 0.1 entry; a stop to the first tick over. Same in the strategy form's
  preview (`exitPrice`). The levels the desk trades (185% / 99%, the 30 Sep
  fills) are unchanged. `trading/exit-levels.test.ts` keeps the sweep.

Left as they are, for the owner to decide: the backstop's distance (stop plus
its distance from the entry, so a 60 stop on a 20 entry rests at 100 on the
mark), the backstop being a stop limit (it can miss a gap past its 15% slack),
and the 15-second confirmation on the offer. Each trades a slower or wider exit
against the false exits of 26 and 28 Sep ([0006](../decisions/0006-target-is-a-price-stop-is-an-exit.md)).
Tests: `trading/sibling-cancel.test.ts` (new), `trading/stop-orders.test.ts`,
`strategy/StrategyPanel.test.tsx`.
