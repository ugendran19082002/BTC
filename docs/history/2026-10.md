## Faster to the order, and to the signal: six changes that trade nothing differently (4 Oct 2026)

The owner asked for the entry caught sooner. Each of these removes a wait; none changes which signal is taken,
which strike is sold, or at what price.

- **The leverage is not asked for before every entry.** It is a setting on the product at Delta, and the desk
  set it in front of each order -- a whole round trip to set the number it had set a minute before. It is now
  remembered per product for ten minutes (`LEVERAGE_HELD_MS`), and forgotten the moment an entry is refused.
- **A contract is made ready before its order is due** (`engine.warm`, the runner's `warmFor`): when a signal
  appears that a strategy will take at the zone, the contract it would sell now has its product looked up and its
  leverage set. Nothing is claimed or placed; the strike is still chosen on the board as it stands when the zone
  fills.
- **A working entry is not left to the loop** (`trading/entry-watch.ts`): a trade with its entry order working,
  or filled and without its target yet, is polled four times a second on its own -- at most two trades a look, and
  none while the desk has used half of Delta's quota. (A correction on the way: the journal's fill time is when the
  desk noticed the fill, not Delta's own, so the 1.6 s measured from fill to target was the target routine and
  its retry -- Delta, asked a moment after a fill, sometimes does not show the position yet -- not the poll.)
- **The perp's own prints drive the watchers** (`onPerpPrint`): the SL and TGT watch looks on every print as well
  as every 100 ms, and the zone grader on every print, not more than once in 50 ms.
- **The signal run goes as soon as the candle is final** (the owner's sequence: close, wait a second, read, verify,
  run, or try again shortly). It waited three seconds after every close. Now it looks at one second and at two,
  and runs at once if the venue's candles are exactly what the desk's own tape says the minute was -- close,
  high, low and volume, and every longer candle closing on the same boundary ending at the same trade
  (`closeVerified`). Otherwise the settled read at three seconds runs, as before. The methods read the venue's
  candles either way.
- **Checked in real time** on a paper copy reading Delta's live data: the venue's 1m candle was exactly the
  tape's minute one second after the close on four minutes of four (close, high, low and volume), and the signal
  run went early on three of five minutes -- the first two could not, the tape having only just connected. Signals
  were ready about 2 s after the close on an early run, against 4.1 s on the settled one. The run's own
  calculation held the thread for 0.14 to 0.23 s.
- **Not built**: a thread of its own for the signal run (the gauges now say how long it holds the thread; it is
  decided on that), and Delta's private fill stream (it cannot be proven here without the account's keys).

## A stop was acted on late: the fast watch on a signal trade's SL and TGT (4 Oct 2026)

The owner asked for speed on the signal path. Measured first, on the day's own records:

| Step | Median | Worst decile |
|---|---|---|
| Candle closes -> signal seen | 3.0 s | by design: the read runs 3 s after the close |
| Signal seen -> order sent (every strategy enters at the zone) | 7.9 s | 239 s, waiting for the perp to reach the zone |
| Order sent -> first fill | 21 s | 48 s: the order rests at the offer and holds at the mid while the spread is over 15% |
| Fill -> target resting at Delta | 1.6 s | 2.3 s |
| Close sent -> filled | 1.0 s | 1.5 s |
| **Perp through the SL -> the desk acts** | **10 pts past** | **82 pts past; 185 at worst** |

The last row was the fault. A signal trade's SL and TGT are perp levels, judged inside the engine's poll -- and the
service polled the open trades one after another, each poll three round trips to Delta. With twenty trades open, a
stop waited behind nineteen trades that had nothing to do: 30 of 95 stops in three days were acted on more than 25
points late, and five trades stopped together at 02:36 on 4 Oct were closed over 19 seconds, 87 to 100 points past.

- **The fast watch** (`trading/underlying-watch.ts`): the perp's last trade, already in memory off the tape, against
  every open signal trade's levels ten times a second. A trade through its level is handed to the engine at once
  (`exitOnUnderlying`): the engine's own check and its own close, in the trade's own queue, so the watch and the
  poll make one close between them. Trades on one contract are closed one after another, as before; different
  contracts at the same time. The poll still judges the same levels on every pass -- if the watch stops, the desk is
  as it was.
- **Two per-second reads held until they can change.** The paper grader's working rows (528 rows, 277 kB, every
  second) are held until the entry tables are written; the day's booked P&L (every trade of the day, every second)
  until the journal is. Both are exact: every writer moves the version the held answer is kept against. Together
  they were most of the 0.8 MB a second the database sent the API.
- **The database given room** (deploy/docker-compose.yml): two CPUs and 1.5 GB instead of one and 512 MB, 512 MB of
  buffers for a 440 MB database, 16 MB a sort, and `pg_stat_statements` loaded. A backup's 18-second COPY had the
  signal path's reads waiting behind it on the one CPU. **The database restarts on the deploy that carries this.**
- **The zone entry sees its fill four times a second.** A strategy that enters "at the zone" sends its option order
  when the live grader sees the perp trade into the zone; the grader looked once a second, so up to a second was
  added to every such entry. It now looks every 250 ms (`LIVE_GRADE_MS`), never two looks at once. The watch on SL
  and TGT looks every 100 ms -- as often as the screens are sent the perp's last trade.
- **Every screen checked at every width.** `app/web/scripts/responsive-check.mjs` now walks all eight screens at
  360, 390, 768, 1024, 1366 and 1920 px and fails if the page scrolls sideways or anything outside a scroller of
  its own reaches past the edge. All pass, on a paper desk with empty tables; its stale "Collapse all" step went.
- **Not changed, and why**: the 3 s wait after a candle closes (the engine reads Delta's finished candle; reading
  the tape instead is a different input and needs measuring first); the 21 s to fill (that is each strategy's own
  "rest at the offer, not into a wide bid" setting); Redis (one process holds its caches in memory -- a second
  server would add a hop, not remove one).

## A desk-wide cap on open trades, and the TGT distance filter (4 Oct 2026)

The owner: "at most open at once" is per strategy; with a small account and several strategies it adds up past the
margin, Delta refuses the order and fines it. One number over all of them is needed -- and the SL-distance filter
wants a twin for the target.

- **`signal_max_open`** (a setting; `POST /api/strategies/max-open`, 0-500, 0 = no limit): `takeSignal` skips a
  signal while the desk already holds that many open trades, positions and working orders, whichever strategy or
  hand opened them -- after the strategy's own limit, which still applies. The Signal Strategies card shows it
  left of Auto-trading, with how many are open against it; typed, saved on leaving the field.
- **Held to the strategies' own sum**: the limit cannot be set above what the switched-on strategies allow between
  them (refused with the sum, by the screen and by the server); unset, the field shows that sum. Under the header a
  line adds the strategies up -- entries, lots, margin against what is free, and the worst case under the limit --
  and each strategy's row says what is in use of its own limit: open now, lots, margin.
- **A wrong comparison, caught the same day**: the header first held the *whole* margin requirement against the
  free balance. Delta's free balance is already net of what the open positions use, so they were counted twice --
  the live desk read "272% of free" with twelve positions open. It now holds only what is *still to open* against
  what is free. Display only: no order was placed or refused on that figure.
- **The limits under load** (`e2e/signal-strategy.test.ts`): a ticket trade takes a place under the cap; five
  signals in one moment with two places left place exactly two; a fill at the zone is held to the cap; a cancelled
  entry frees its place; a strategy's own limit holds inside the desk's.
- **The summary as three tiles** -- Strategies allow, In use now, Still to open -- each with its number, a bar
  where it is a share, and for the last a word and an icon (Fits / Tight from 80% of what is free / More than is
  free), never colour alone. Lots and "at most open" are fields on each strategy's row as well as in its form.
- **The summary, held to everything that stops an entry** (the same afternoon): a signal was refused for the
  desk's limit on lots short (110 of 116) while the summary called the place free. "Still to open" is now bound
  by that limit too and says so; the margin in use is Delta's own figure where Delta gives it; what is still
  needed is priced at the dearer of the model and Delta's rate per lot. The card's foot says which build is
  running (`BUILD_TAG`, from the image tag). Display and status only: no order path changed.
- **The rule in force now** on each strategy's row: which block the clock is in, until when, what it sells, and
  what follows (`blockNow`, the screen's copy of `strikePickAt`).
- **`signal.minTgtPts`**: per timeframe, the least distance from the perp entry to the target the trade exits at.
  Beside `minSlPts` on the Signals tab, both 0 (off) until typed; a signal failing either is `skipped` with the
  prices and the distance, both when both fail.
- **Tests**: the cap and the TGT filter in `e2e/signal-strategy.test.ts` and `signal-sl-distance.test.ts` (server);
  `SignalStrategiesCard.test.tsx` and the distance cases in `SignalStrategyForm.test.tsx` (screen).

## A signal strategy's strike rule by time of day (4 Oct 2026)

The owner: one premium for a 23-hour window is the wrong number for most of it -- cut the window into 4-hour
blocks (the length editable), each with its own premium or strike.

- **`strikeBlocks`** on a signal strategy's config (JSONB, so no migration; absent is one rule all window):
  `[{ at, strikeRule: premium | strict, strikeStep, premium }]`. Before the first block the strategy's own
  rule is in force -- the same reading as `targetSteps` (`strikePickAt`, `strategy/types.ts`).
- **The runner** (`takeSignal`) picks the strike by the block the clock is in when the order is sent, and the
  signal's row says which block. A clock strategy never carries blocks: the save drops them.
- **The form**: Strike & lots -> "Different strike rule by time of day". Split every N hours (4 by default),
  a row per block with its start time and its rule, add / remove, and "Split again" when the window was moved.
- **The distance rule and its else strike** (`premium.minOtm`, `premium.elseOtm`, on the rule and on each block;
  clock strategies too): the premium's pick stands at the rule's strike or further out; nearer, or with no pick,
  the rule has failed and the else strike -- its own number, equal or different -- is sold (`pickStrike`,
  `strategy/select.ts`). The row says "rule failed: … — went to the else strike OTM n", and the trade history shows
  it under the strike. Stored only when set.
- **The Strike & lots tab**: two tick boxes, "Same strike rule all the time" (default) and "Different strike rule
  by time of day", each with its own section; block 1 is the strategy's own rule, edited in its row. The premium
  reads "≥ Greater or equal" / "≤ Less or equal". "Its own minimum premium" moved to the bottom.
- **SL distance per timeframe** (`signal.minSlPts`, without the chain): a signal whose SL is nearer the perp entry
  than its timeframe's points is `skipped`, with both prices and the distance in its row (`takeSignal`).
- **Tests**: `strike-blocks.test.ts`, `signal-sl-distance.test.ts`, the rule and else cases in `select.test.ts` and
  six cases in `e2e/signal-strategy.test.ts` (server); `strategy-blocks.test.ts`, the blocks, distance-rule and
  SL-distance cases in `SignalStrategyForm.test.tsx` and the strike-rule cases in `SignalTradeHistory.test.tsx`.
- **A test that only passed after 10:00 IST**: "enter at the zone" stamped its fill with the machine's clock
  while the runner ran at a fixed 10:00, so before 10:00 the fill read as hours old and was skipped. It now
  stamps the fill with the runner's clock.

## Delta Exchange lookup optimization & Error logging best practices (3 Oct 2026)

- **Delta direct client ID endpoint (`GET /v2/orders/client_order_id/{id}`)**:
  Orders are now queried directly by Delta's dedicated client ID endpoint first, resolving both open and filled states in a single roundtrip.
- **Contract-specific history using numeric `product_ids`**:
  Deep order search now supplies `product_ids={productId}` (the documented parameter Delta respects) alongside `product_symbols`.
- **Throttling deep order searches (`deepMiss`)**:
  To protect Delta's 20,000 units / 5-min quota, missed deep searches are cached and rate-throttled for 30 seconds (`DEEP_RETRY_MS = 30_000`).
- **Critical fault logging on 5xx (`worthLogging`)**:
  Fixed bug where `worthLogging(500, true)` returned `false`. A 5xx is always an unhandled fault and must never be suppressed from the error log.
- **UI Error Boundary crash loop protection**:
  Added a 2-second retry debounce (`RETRY_COOLDOWN_MS = 2_000`) in `ErrorBoundary.tsx`. If a component crashes immediately after clicking "try again", the boundary locks with a clean user message instead of looping infinitely.
- **Browser error dedup map pruning**:
  Replaced destructive `sent.clear()` on reaching 200 items with TTL-based timestamp pruning in `app/web/src/lib/report-error.ts`, preserving recent fingerprints during error bursts.
- **ErrorLog startup prune**:
  `ErrorLog` now prunes the table on initialization/boot after migrations rather than waiting for 50 writes.

## Twelve trades "closed" at a restart; targets the running desk never saw fill (2 Oct 2026)

The owner: 88,800 CE trades DONE with "bought back at —". Cross-checked on the backups either side of the
22:06 restart (trade tables only, in a scratch database on the test server, dropped after): fifteen trades
open before, three after.

- **The running desk could not see a filled target on a busy account.** It looks a target up by client id;
  Delta ignores that filter on its order endpoints, so a filled order is found only among the account's
  newest twenty. With fifteen trades and signal strategies placing all day, a target that filled was past them
  in minutes, and the trade read "open" until the restart -- whose check reads the *contract's* history (a
  filter Delta honours) and found nine target fills at 2.20 at once. The lookup now falls back to the
  contract's history (`getOrderByClientId(cid, symbol)`).
- **Three 88,800 CE trades were written off on one read** ("exchange says 0, we had -3"), with no fill to
  explain it. One read is no longer enough: flat with nothing to explain it is read again two seconds later,
  and either not written off or written off with an alarm.
- **No BTC price after a restart.** The desk learnt spot only from the Live screen's board; a no-stop signal
  trade was refused eight minutes after a deploy. Spot is now the live perp's last trade when fresh.
- **"Closed manually" over a perp SL hit**: the Orders row read only the filling order; a desk close is a
  market order whatever the reason. Now named from the close reason (from the journal for older trades).
- **Positions say an exit asked for and not resting at Delta**, with Delta's reason.
- **The Telegram log**: every message, sent / failed / held back, on Settings. Only failures were kept, in
  the error log -- and the entry alerts' silence today was their switches, off since 1 Oct 14:54.

## Signal history audit: the paper grader's blind seconds, and its two clocks (1 Oct 2026)

The owner: "signal history R&D". A code audit of how a TRADE is written,
graded and summed (the live rows need a production read this session could
not make). Three grading faults, each biasing the record; fixed going forward.
Rows graded before the fix keep their old result.

- **The rest of an event's minute was graded by nobody.** The live tick grader
  closes the minute of a fill, TP1 or TGT2 to the candle grader (so a candle
  never replays prices from before the event), but then began its own next pass
  at the following minute. A stop seconds after the fill -- the commonest way a
  setup fails -- went unseen, and a later TP1 could be written as the win. A
  runner back to breakeven in its TP1 minute was missed the same way. The tape
  now carries on from its own last trade inside a minute it closed itself
  (`live-grade.ts`); a restart, with no cursor, still never goes back over it.
- **The fill window let one minute too many through**: a setup could fill in
  the minute starting at `fillBy`, after the screen's counter had run out. The
  window is now checked first.
- **The time-out came a minute late**: a stop or TP1 in the minute after the
  hold ran out beat the time-out, which then took that minute's close. Time is
  now judged first, at the open of that minute -- the price when it ran out --
  for the trade and for a runner.

Read the record with two things in mind: R is before fees (`entry-010`), and a
time-out at exactly breakeven counts as a loss in the win rate. Tests:
`entry/live-grade.test.ts` (3 new), `entry/paper.test.ts` (4 new, 1 corrected).

## Exit audit: the losing exit confirmed off the book, the close-watched stop by time (1 Oct 2026)

The owner: "ask, bid, entry, exit, TGT, SL -- best practice, full R&D and
audit". A code audit of where each price is used, how the two exits are kept
in step, and partial fills. Partial fills were already right (exits re-anchor
on the size-weighted fill every poll). Three things were not:

- **The losing exit was struck off the record without being confirmed off the
  book.** `cancelSiblings` wrote `sibling_cancelled` whatever happened -- a
  lookup that failed, a cancel Delta did not honour -- and a flat trade is never
  polled again, so a reduce-only order could rest on with the desk believing it
  gone. With two trades on one contract (0011) it would buy back the other
  trade's contracts. Now a leg comes off the record only once the venue confirms
  it; an unconfirmed one is retried by the trade's poll while it is open and by
  `sweepLeftovers` (every service step) once it is done, and a minute of it is a
  phone alarm. `cancelAndVerify` no longer reads a failed lookup as "gone". A
  close still goes out when its exits cannot be confirmed: a stop that does not
  exit is worse than a reduce-only order resting a moment longer. The list is in
  memory; a restart forgets it.
- **The close-watched stop judged the wrong bar** when no bar was forming:
  `lastClosedBar` took the second-to-last bar on the assumption that the last
  is always the one forming. A thin option with no trade yet this minute has no
  forming bar, so the bar that had just closed through the stop was skipped. It
  now picks the newest bar whose minute is over, by time.
- **The scheduler switch did not say it stops the exit times.** Off now says so
  on the strategy tab, and that the target and stop at Delta still work.
- **An exit could land on the entry price.** A sweep of every entry from 0.1 to
  100 (and half-tick averages) against every percentage and distance found
  rounding to the 0.1 tick putting a target on the entry (any 0.1 entry; any
  distance under a tick) or a stop on it (a 10% stop on 0.1-0.4). A target there
  buys back at the price sold, fees paid twice; a stop there is reached as the
  entry fills. A target now goes to the first tick under the entry, or is none
  at a 0.1 entry; a stop to the first tick over. Same in the strategy form's
  preview (`exitPrice`). The levels the desk trades (185% / 99%, the 30 Sep
  fills) are unchanged. `trading/exit-levels.test.ts` keeps the sweep.

Left as they are, for the owner to decide: the backstop's distance (stop plus
its distance from the entry, so a 60 stop on a 20 entry rests at 100 on the
mark), the backstop being a stop limit (it can miss a gap past its 15% slack),
and the 15-second confirmation on the offer. Each trades a slower or wider exit
against the false exits of 26 and 28 Sep ([0006](../decisions/0006-target-is-a-price-stop-is-an-exit.md)).
Tests: `trading/sibling-cancel.test.ts` (new), `trading/stop-orders.test.ts`,
`strategy/StrategyPanel.test.tsx`.
